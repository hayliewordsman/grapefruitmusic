# Grapefruit Audio 6.0 beta 1 — build 65

Grapefruit Music & Podcasts becomes Grapefruit Audio: Music, Podcasts, and Audiobooks. 5.2 was not
released: its two betas' worth of work (tab menus, OPML, podcast speed) is in this beta. The app ID
(`io.github.hayliewordsman.grapefruitmusic`) and signing key are unchanged, so it updates 5.1 in place.

## The name, the icon, the wordmark

- `app_name` and `launcher_name` are "Grapefruit Audio"; the full name is the About page's wordmark
  and the README's title. Crash reports, the FM radio report, the OPML export's title, the local
  files permission message and the Settings and About footers use the new name. Kept on purpose:
  the Jellyfin client name ("Grapefruit Music", so servers don't list Grapefruit as a new device)
  and the FLAC vendor string written by the tag editor.
- `tools/brand/logo.py`: `headphones()` draws a plum (#4A1622) band over the slice and a cup each
  side (cushion #6E2434), inside the circle and inside an adaptive icon's 66dp safe zone (the band's
  top at 0.44 of the picture's diameter from its centre, against the safe zone's 0.458). It's in
  `grapefruit()` (launcher, About icon, Android 12 splash icon, logos) and `adaptive_layer()`.
- The stacked logo (splash screen, README) is "Grapefruit Audio" ("Grapefruit" in the name colour)
  over "Music, Podcasts, and Audiobooks"; the horizontal logo is the grapefruit and "Grapefruit Audio".
- The About page's wordmark is two lines in Geist Pixel Triangle: "Grapefruit Audio" (30sp,
  "Grapefruit" in the accent) and "Music, Podcasts, and Audiobooks" (17sp), each shrunk to fit a
  narrow screen; TalkBack reads the full name once.
- The wordmark font subset (the letters of both lines) is now written to the app's own
  `app/src/main/assets/fonts/`, which takes the place of `grapefruit-ui`'s copy in the app's APK, so
  the shared library (and Grapefruit TV, which has its own wordmark) is untouched.
- Not changed yet: the F-Droid store listing (fastlane title, descriptions and icon) and the official
  F-Droid recipe's name and `Binaries` pattern. They change with 6.0 stable, so the listing doesn't
  show a beta's name and icon over 5.1.

## The APK's name

- The release workflow (`fdroid-repo.yml`) names the release asset `grapefruit-audio-<version>.apk`
  (5.1 was `grapefruit-<version>.apk`). The official F-Droid recipe's `Binaries` line must follow
  at 6.0 stable.

## Tabs

- Music (was Library), Radio, Podcasts, Audiobooks, TV & Movies, Search, Settings. Recently Added
  in Music lists albums and playlists only (no podcast subscriptions; they're in Podcasts).
- TV & Movies is shown only when Grapefruit TV is installed, and opens it rather than a page:
  `findTvApp` looks for `io.github.hayliewordsman.grapefruitfast` (Grapefruit FAST, Grapefruit TV
  2.x and Grapefruit TV & Movies 3.0 beta 1 all use it, checked on its branch), then for any
  `io.github.hayliewordsman.*` launcher app whose label starts "Grapefruit TV", in case a later one
  moves. The manifest's `<queries>` lets Android 11 and later see it. Checked again on every resume,
  so installing or removing it shows or hides the tab.
- With seven tabs the tab bar sizes its padding to the room and shows the chosen tab's name only
  where the widest name fits.
- Tab menus (from the 5.2 work): tapping the tab showing, or touching and holding any tab but
  Settings, opens its chips as a menu rising from the button (beside the rail when sideways);
  Search's menu is its filters. It opens below the button when there's more room there.

## Audiobooks

- Formats: whatever Android plays, read for chapters by Grapefruit. M4B/M4A/MP4 (QuickTime chapter
  track, or Nero `chpl`), MP3 (ID3v2.3/2.4 `CHAP` with `TIT2`), FLAC, Ogg Vorbis and Opus
  (`CHAPTERnnn` comments; Opus plays on Android 5 and later), AAC and WAV. A book that's a folder of
  files takes each file as a chapter, unless the files have chapters inside. Chapter parsing seeks
  through the file (RandomAccessFile) rather than reading it whole. `ChaptersTest` covers MP4 track
  and `chpl`, ID3 CHAP, FLAC and Ogg comments, and names from file names.
- Libro.fm (https://libro.fm/guide, and its help page on downloading to a computer) sells DRM-free
  audiobooks as MP3s in zip files (long books in several parts) or as one M4B. Add takes either:
  zips are unzipped into a folder named for the book (parts of one book go into the same folder;
  paths that would escape the folder are refused), and free space is checked first.
- Books are found in the shared `Audiobooks` folder on the device and in Grapefruit's own folder,
  where imported books go: a folder of audio files is one book, and each M4B, or an audio file
  directly in a books folder, is a book of its own. Titles, authors and lengths come from the files
  (MediaMetadataRetriever), with the cover from a picture beside the book or inside its first file.
  They're kept in the database (version 6, `audiobooks`) with a signature of the files' names,
  sizes and dates, so only new or changed books are read again; books whose files are gone are
  forgotten. The shared Audiobooks folder is kept out of Music's On This Device.
- The tab: Add and Libro.fm buttons, the Libro.fm suggestion ("DRM-free M4B and MP3 files that play
  here, and every purchase supports a local bookstore"), Continue Listening (started, not finished,
  latest first) and All Audiobooks, with progress, time left, length and chapter count. Touching and
  holding a book: Play from the Start, Mark Finished / Not Finished, Remove (books added with Add).
- Playing: the book is one queue, a part each, called by the book's title (mini player, notification,
  lock screen). The place is saved every 15 seconds and on pause, and played from on return; the
  last part's end marks the book finished, and a finished book starts over. Up Next, Lyrics,
  Karaoke and Live Mode don't apply; shuffle and repeat are hidden, and the player ignores them for
  a book. The skip buttons go back and ahead 15 seconds.
- Now Playing shows the chapter as the title, "Author · Chapter n of m" under it, and Chapters in
  place of Up Next (when there's more than one): the chapters with where each starts in the book,
  the one playing in the accent; tapping one goes there, across parts.

## Playback speed

- 0.8×, 1×, 1.2×, 1.5×, 1.75× and 2×, kept separately for music, podcasts and audiobooks, from the
  Speed button in Now Playing (it shows the speed when it isn't 1×). The time left is divided by the
  speed.
- `TimeStretch`: WSOLA in integer arithmetic (40 ms sequences, 10 ms cross-fades, a 15 ms search for
  the best match, correlation on every fourth sample then refined to the sample), between
  decoding and the effects in Grapefruit's own engine (DspEngine), which plays whenever the speed
  isn't 1×. The position reported is the song's, not the output's; changing speed seeks to the
  current place so nothing queued at the old speed is heard. About 160× real time on a desktop JVM
  for 44.1 kHz stereo. `TimeStretchTest`: length at each speed, a tone's pitch kept, mono, very
  short sounds, timing.

## Lyrics timing

- With synced lyrics showing, a Timing pill at the bottom right: Earlier by 0.5 s, Later by 0.5 s,
  As Timed. The offset (up to 30 s either way) is kept for that song (database `lyrics_timing`)
  and used for the highlight and for tapping a line.

## Podcast subscriptions: OPML

- Podcasts › Subscriptions has Import and Export. Import reads an OPML file from another podcast app
  (any outline with a feed in `xmlUrl`, or `url`, nested or not) and subscribes to the feeds not
  already followed; Export writes Grapefruit's subscriptions as OPML 2.0. `OpmlTest` covers both and odd files.
