# Grapefruit Music 4.1 beta 4 — build 42

Rebuilds Live Mode's sound and timing, and fixes the scrubber and podcast times with effects on.

## Live Mode

Beta 3's audience was a half-second noise loop (it repeated twice a second), random white-noise
crackle every few tens of milliseconds, two echo taps with feedback (a flutter, not a room), the
whole 240Hz to 3kHz band delayed by 65 to 150ms as the "singalong", and 8-bit 12kHz cheers that
started on top of the song's first notes and after any silent tail at its end. Replaced by:

- `LiveCrowd` / `assets/live_crowd.bin` (1.3MB): a new synthesized crowd from
  `tools/live/render_crowd.py`: modelled hand claps (thousands of clappers, each with their own
  resonance, rate, jitter and place), hundreds of formant-synthesized cheering voices, whistles, a
  synthesized stadium response. 16kHz bed loop (distant crowd only, nothing distinctive to notice
  repeating), 22.05kHz opening cheer and closing ovation, mu-law. No recordings, libraries or trained
  models. The ovation's long-term spectrum was compared with a public-domain applause field
  recording (archive.org `aporee_47869_54396`, measured only, not included): within a few dB from
  300Hz to 10kHz.
- `LiveStage`: the crowd cheers two seconds before the first note when a song starts from the
  beginning (DspEngine holds the first decoded audio back; the time shows 0:00 meanwhile), carries
  on under the intro and dies down; a distant crowd through the song (level follows the song's
  loudness); a cheer when the song lifts by 3dB and holds it (at most every 35 s); the lead vocal
  sung by a six-voice crowd (30 to 120ms late, about 15 cents of drift, dulled, mostly
  reverberant); the song and crowd in an eight-line FDN stadium reverb (2.4 s) instead of echoes.
- The ovation starts when the music ends, not after the file: a steady fall in the last 15 s (a
  fade-out), 9dB under the recent peak (briefly in the last 3 s, over 1.2 s before then, so a break
  before the last chorus doesn't count), or the end of the file. In a long fade it's held back so it
  lasts past the end. After the file ends, the rest of it plays before the next song.
- The crowd sings the vocal from Karaoke Mode's separator, which now runs for Live Mode too (loaded
  before the first Live song starts). If the effects keep the audio thread over 65% busy for two
  2-second windows while the separator runs for Live Mode alone, Live Mode stops using it for the
  run and follows the song's centre instead (Karaoke Mode itself is never turned down).
- Song-dependent processing runs at about 11kHz. Desktop JVM, per frame: Live Mode with the
  separator costs 1.05x Karaoke Mode with the JIT, 1.22x interpreted; Live Mode without it 0.11x
  and 0.23x of Karaoke Mode. Not a JP5S measurement.

## Scrubber and podcast times

- With an effect on (DspEngine), a seek flushed the AudioTrack and kept counting from its playback
  head. On Android 4.x a flushed track can keep its old position (the flush is applied later, or not
  at all if it plays again first), so the time ran ahead of the audio: the scrubber landed past
  where it was let go and went on from there, podcasts saved that time as their place, and reached
  the end early (stuck at -0:00) while the episode played on. A seek now opens a new AudioTrack.
- The scrubber no longer uses the lift-off point (some screens report it well off), doesn't jump
  when the thumb is picked up off-centre, and a tap on the thumb doesn't seek.
- DspEngine no longer stops the time at an estimated length before the end; at the end it takes
  the decoder's real end. Player.durationMs() is never shorter than the position.

## Validation

- Local Gradle (JDK 21, AGP 8.10.1): assembleDebug, assembleRelease, testDebugUnitTest, lintDebug
  passed. 163 unit tests, 0 failures. Lint: no errors; no new warnings in changed files.
- New `LiveStageTest` (19 tests): exact dry output at 0% (with and without the separator, after its
  delay), cheering before the first note and dying down, no cheer when starting part-way, seeking
  into the opening, the ovation at a fade-out (and lasting past the file), at music stopping before
  a silent tail, not at a short break, at the end of the file without a length, a cheer at a big
  moment, the crowd singing only when there's a voice, strength, reverberation decaying to silence,
  bounded stereo at 8 to 96kHz, chunking, mono files and Mono Audio with Karaoke Mode, and the slow
  device fallback.
- Not tested on a device: the touch changes, the AudioTrack behaviour after seeks, and how Live Mode
  sounds and performs on the JP5S.
